## Implemented validation contract

- Retain the safe core; measure an extended halo against a wider robust local background rather than extrapolating the contaminated core annulus.
- Validate narrow spike directions through paired, sustained radial signal and transverse contrast. Rotation must not depend on image diagonals. A lone ridge or nearby nebular knot is not sufficient evidence.
- Feather both ends and sides of extended components, and use identical two-dimensional weights for mask display and rendering. Combine overlapping support once.
- Keep explicit neighbor, invalid-pixel, edge and cancellation handling. A bounded or ambiguous component must not be labeled complete.
- Check genuine correction beyond 32 pixels, background preservation between spikes, smooth transitions and unchanged zero-strength rendering on known synthetic scenes.
- Revisit sources 771 and 702 at several strengths; retain the existing skipped cases as controls. Real-data cutouts remain development evidence, not proof of general astrophysical separation.

The initial supported spike morphology is a rotated, orthogonal four-arm cross. Detection requires paired transverse contrast and declining connected support on all four arms. A single ridge, an unbounded arm, invalid samples or an intersecting mapped neighbor can leave all spike extensions unavailable while a safe core remains usable. The halo and spikes are optional independent components; a successful preview is not a claim that every component is complete.

Background estimates use a robust plane in a wider annulus, with catalog-neighbor exclusion and a check for unresolved radial structure. The detector uses a fresh outer noise estimate, rather than the halo-contaminated core noise. Circular support requires multiple radial sectors, decline and quiet outer samples. The renderer preserves the core's existing background and blends toward the outer plane across the core feather. It compresses observed positive excess; it does not synthesize detector-lost flux.
